IllusionGameSelfAssociation - LNR: participant-level indices and their correlates

The merged fit (analysis/models/lnr.rds) at the participant level: each participant’s own indices, how they hang together across illusions, and how much of the group differences age, language, student status and the questionnaires account for. The population-level group differences are in 3_lnr_groups.qmd; the indices are defined in analysis/server/marginals.R. Render from the repo root (about 2 min):

quarto render analysis/4_lnr_correlates.qmd

Three caveats apply to everything below.

  • Shrinkage. The participant estimates are shrunk towards their group’s mean, since Group is in the model. That flattens every within-group slope, so what a covariate accounts for is understated. Adjusting properly needs a refit with the covariate in the model. The same regressions on raw summaries of the trials, which are not shrunk, are the check.
  • Five participants mix poorly in the 16-chain fit (AGENTS.md, status step 3): S092, S088, S116, S003 and S097, with participant-level Rhat up to 1.27. S092 sits in a second mode in two of the 16 chains, with much more of its trials in the outlier component. Their estimates are less reliable than the others’; leaving those two chains out changes none of the population results.
  • Exploratory. Several indices, three illusions and six covariates make many cells. The illusion magnitude is the primary index; everything else is descriptive.

Every index is taken at mid task difficulty (Task_Difficulty = 0) and, for the correlates, averaged over the three illusions per posterior draw.

Participant indices

Per participant and illusion, from 1,000 evenly spaced draws with the participant’s random effects in:

  • With no illusion: discriminability (nu0 − nu1), speed ((nu0 + nu1) / 2), non-decision time and P(error).
  • The effect of the illusion, from strength 0 to 1: on discriminability and on P(error).
  • Precision: the change in discriminability from mid difficulty to the hardest trials (negative: harder trials lower it).
  • Illusion magnitude: the illusion effect on discriminability over the precision term. Both are negative, so it is positive: how many units of Task_Difficulty one unit of Illusion_Strength costs. It is the PSE shift in stimulus units, and it is unchanged by anything that scales the whole surface (marginals.R, igsa_index()).

What the covariates account for

Each participant’s index, averaged over the illusions, is regressed on Group alone, then on Group and a covariate, once per posterior draw. Each draw’s coefficients are themselves drawn from their sampling distribution, so the intervals carry both the uncertainty of the participant estimates and that of a regression on about a hundred people. Age is per 10 years; the questionnaire scores are per standard deviation across the analysed participants. The MEQ-30 exists for users only, so its regressions compare Recent with Past.

The raw summaries of the trials, regressed the same way, are the unshrunk check: the 10th percentile of correct RTs (the fast tail, which tracks non-decision time), and the error rates on facilitating-or-no-illusion and on conflicting trials.

# One coefficient vector per posterior draw, drawn from its sampling
# distribution around that draw's least-squares fit, on the complete cases
# of the design.
regress <- function(Q, formula) {
  vars <- all.vars(formula)
  keep <- stats::complete.cases(info[vars])
  d <- droplevels(info[keep, ])
  X <- stats::model.matrix(formula, d)
  Y <- t(Q[, keep, drop = FALSE])
  fit <- qr(X)
  B <- qr.coef(fit, Y)
  s2 <- colSums(qr.resid(fit, Y)^2) / (nrow(X) - ncol(X))
  L <- chol(solve(crossprod(X)))
  B <- B + t(L) %*% matrix(stats::rnorm(length(B)), nrow(B)) * rep(sqrt(s2), each = nrow(B))
  # Without Never (users only), Past is the reference.
  if (!"Never" %in% levels(d$Group)) rownames(B) <- sub("^GroupRecent$", "GroupRecentPast", rownames(B))
  list(B = B, n = nrow(X))
}
term_labels <- c(term_labels, GroupRecentPast = "Recent - Past")

set.seed(1)
coefs <- do.call(rbind, lapply(names(outcomes), function(qn) {
  do.call(rbind, lapply(names(designs), function(k) {
    r <- regress(outcomes[[qn]], designs[[k]])
    do.call(rbind, lapply(intersect(names(term_labels), rownames(r$B)), function(tm) {
      v <- r$B[tm, ]
      data.frame(Quantity = qn, Design = k, N = r$n, Term = term_labels[[tm]], Median = stats::median(v),
                 CI_low = stats::quantile(v, 0.025), CI_high = stats::quantile(v, 0.975),
                 pd = max(mean(v > 0), mean(v < 0)), row.names = NULL)
    }))
  }))
}))
